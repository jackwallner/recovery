## I finished a workout and there is no countdown

Walks, yoga, and mobility work are treated as active recovery and never
start a countdown. Other sessions still need enough duration and
intensity to produce a meaningful cardiovascular load. Recharge+ also
compares that load with your own recent training, so a session below the
quietest part of your history may be skipped.

## My lifting session was scored as easy

The Apple Watch optical sensor loses the heart-rate signal under grip
and bar contact, so a heavy session can look like an easy one. When that
happens Recharge asks one question, Light, Moderate, or Hard, on the
phone or on the wrist, and recalculates from your answer.

## Functional and hybrid training is scored wrong

Apple Health reports these sessions the same way it reports other
functional training, so Recharge cannot tell the exact format
automatically. Settings › Model › Functional and hybrid training sets
the default curve for ambiguous sessions. Recharge+ also lets you
correct a session's intensity from its entry in History.

## The complication is not updating

watchOS budgets how often a complication may refresh, so the countdown
can lag by a few minutes. Opening the Recharge app on either device
brings it back in step. Settings › Watch complication also changes what
it displays: the hours countdown, the estimated ready time, or the
state.

## What does countdown complete mean?

A completed countdown means the training-load estimate has run out. It
is not a statement about muscle repair, illness, injury risk, or whether
you are fit to train.

## Managing your subscription

Recharge+ is managed in Settings › Apple ID › Subscriptions on your
iPhone. The Lifetime purchase is one-time and does not renew. Use
Restore in the app's Settings if a purchase does not appear on a new
device.

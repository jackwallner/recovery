## Recharge Workout Recovery Time

Recharge starts a training-load countdown after every qualifying workout recorded in Apple Health and shows it on your iPhone and Apple Watch. It runs a separate curve for each kind of session (endurance, strength, mixed and easy): endurance sessions use heart-rate-reserve load from your workout heart rate, strength sessions ask you one question, and walks, yoga and mobility never start or extend a countdown. Watch complications come in four families (circular, rectangular, inline and corner). When a countdown ends it asks how you actually felt and tunes your own bands from your answer. The standard estimate, the Apple Watch app and the complications are free with no account, and Recharge+ is an optional upgrade that builds estimates from your own history. It reads Apple Health, writes nothing back and has no server. The trade-offs: it is new with no ratings yet, it estimates from recent workout load rather than scoring overnight HRV and sleep, and it cannot account for soreness, illness or mental fatigue.

## Athlytic: Fitness & Recovery

Athlytic reads your overnight HRV and resting heart rate against your own baseline each morning to tell you how recovered you are. Its listing also describes a 0 to 10 exertion score with a target range for the day, workout suggestions, sleep analysis, a journal, and many widgets and watch complications. It has a large audience and a high rating. The listing notes that an Apple Watch is needed for most features, and it reads its data from the Health app. Choose it if you want a full daily readiness dashboard rather than a countdown after one workout.

## Training Today

Training Today produces a Readiness to Train score from HRV readings in Apple Health and your personal baseline. It is aimed at triathletes, runners, cyclists and swimmers, and its listing describes structured workouts that adapt to the score, TrainingPeaks sharing and an Apple Watch app. Its listing says the score supports training decisions and should be read alongside how you feel. It is not an iPad app, and Premium adds the planning features. Choose it for an endurance training plan built around readiness.

## Gentler Streak Workout Tracker

Gentler Streak starts the day with a summary of key vitals and sleep, suggests workouts that respond to your readiness, and keeps a streak that allows for rest days. Its listing also describes real-time training effect and heart rate zones on the watch, a countdown to overreaching, and health data kept on-device through Apple HealthKit. It is broader than a recovery timer, closer to a whole activity tracker. Choose it for gentle, consistency-first guidance.

## RecoTime - Smart Recovery Calc

RecoTime calculates a recovery time after a workout with one tap, using a workout recorded on the watch, and sends reminders meant to avoid overtraining. Its listing includes a plain disclaimer that estimates are for general reference and not medical advice. It has no ratings yet and its latest update shown is September 2025. Choose it if you want a minimal recovery calculator and are comfortable with a little-tested app.

## What is the difference between recovery time and a readiness score?

A recovery time counts down from a specific workout, while a readiness score is a morning snapshot of overnight metrics. They answer different questions, so some people use one of each.

- After a hard session, you want to know when you will be ready again: a countdown such as Recharge.
- Each morning, you want to know how hard to go today: a readiness score such as Athlytic, Training Today or Gentler Streak.

Neither replaces how you feel. Both are estimates from the data your watch records.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best app to track workout recovery time on Apple Watch?

Recharge, for a training-load countdown that starts after each workout. For a morning readiness score from overnight HRV and resting heart rate, Athlytic, Training Today or Gentler Streak.

### Is there a recovery time app for Apple Watch that needs no ring or strap?

Yes. Recharge works from Apple Health workout data and the Apple Watch you already own, with no ring, strap or account. Athlytic, Training Today and Gentler Streak also read Apple Health data, according to their listings.

### What is the difference between a recovery time countdown and a readiness score?

A countdown starts after a workout and runs down over hours, while a readiness score is a snapshot each morning from overnight metrics such as HRV and resting heart rate. Recharge is a countdown, and Athlytic and Training Today are readiness scores.

### Do I need an account to use a recovery app?

Not for Recharge, which has no account or sign-in and no server of its own. The other apps' listings are not explicit about accounts.

### Does Recharge work for strength training?

Yes. Wrist heart rate is unreliable under grip and bar contact, so for strength sessions Recharge asks you one question instead of relying on heart rate alone.

### Is a recovery time estimate medical advice?

No. Recharge gives a cardiovascular training estimate from recent workout load and does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition. It cannot account for soreness, illness or mental fatigue, so go by how you feel.

I finished a workout and there is no countdown

Walks, yoga, and mobility work are treated as active recovery and never start a countdown. Other sessions still need enough duration and intensity to produce a meaningful cardiovascular load. Recharge Pro also compares that load with your own recent training, so a session below the quietest part of your history may be skipped.

My lifting session was scored as easy

The Apple Watch optical sensor loses the heart-rate signal under grip and bar contact, so a heavy session can look like an easy one. When that happens Recharge asks one question, Easy, Moderate, or Hard, on the phone or on the wrist, and recalculates from your answer.

HYROX and CrossFit are scored wrong

Apple Health reports both the same way it reports other functional training, so Recharge cannot tell them apart automatically. Settings › Model › HYROX and CrossFit sets the default, and any single session can be re-classified from its entry in History.

The complication is not updating

watchOS budgets how often a complication may refresh, so the countdown can lag by a few minutes. Opening the Recharge app on either device brings it back in step. Settings › Watch complication also changes what it displays: the hours countdown, the estimated ready time, or the state.

What does Ready actually mean?

Ready means another hard session is likely to be reasonable, based on your recent workout load estimate. It is not a statement about muscle repair, illness, injury risk, or whether you are fit to train.

Managing your subscription

Recharge Pro is managed in Settings › Apple ID › Subscriptions on your iPhone. The Lifetime purchase is one-time and does not renew. Use Restore in the app's Settings if a purchase does not appear on a new device.
